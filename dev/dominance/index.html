Bounded strict dominance

After constructing a workspace, dominance is the inference step that may reduce the solver input. This page explains which signs justify a fixing and how to resume a bounded pass. See the solve walkthrough for what to do with its residual and report.

Select Workspace(model; rules=:dominance) and call propagate! explicitly, or use preprocess(model; rules=:dominance, limits...). For queue-driven degree-0/1/2 exact local elimination (including arbitrary local fields, fill-edge merging/cancellation and optional alternating dominance/elimination cascades), select Workspace(model; rules=:elimination) or call eliminate_low_degree!(ws; cascade_dominance=true, ...). For queue-driven certified pair relations and reversible signed contractions (:interval, :exact and Glover–Lewis–Kochenberger cross-endpoint :glk_pair), select Workspace(model; rules=:relations) or call contract_relations!(ws; max_fill=..., cascade_dominance=..., ...) and inspect active representatives/parities via relation_map(ws). Identity remains the default. Neither path dispatches a solver or implicitly materializes a residual. In pure dominance mode (propagate!(ws)), only strong unary dominance is enabled: every reported value holds in all optima within the current caller scope. Local variable elimination (eliminate_variable!, eliminate_low_degree!) records an EliminationStep in reductions(ws) with its conditional argmin reconstruction table rather than reporting the variable in deductions(ws) as a globally fixed constant; when boundary ties occur (tie_break=:low or :high), persistency(step) is :weak and preserves the optimal objective value and at least one optimal reconstruction without claiming that all original optima or their multiplicities are represented by a single tie choice. Pair relations, weak ties, roof duality and heuristic choices are separate methods.

For minimization of the normalized objective, a binary derivative has bounds aᵢ + Σ min(0,bᵢⱼ) and aᵢ + Σ max(0,bᵢⱼ) over free neighbors. A strictly positive lower bound fixes 0; a strictly negative upper bound fixes 1. For spins the field bounds are aᵢ ± Σ abs(bᵢⱼ), fixing -1/+1 respectively. The boundary field includes fixed neighbors. Maximization and negative scale reverse the sign; positive scale magnitude does not alter optima. Zero scale and equality yield no fixing. The binary/spin rules are equivalent under x=(s+1)/2, rather than independent strengthening methods.

Certified pair relations and Glover–Lewis–Kochenberger rules

For two free variables $u \ne v$ and candidate parity $\sigma \in \{-1, +1\}$ (representing $t_u = \sigma t_v$ in normalized gauged spin coordinates, or $x_u = x_v$ for $\sigma_{\text{orig}} = +1$ and $x_u = 1 - x_v$ for $\sigma_{\text{orig}} = -1$ in binary coordinates), the two violating spin configurations are $V_1 = (t_u = -\sigma, t_v = +1)$ and $V_2 = (t_u = +\sigma, t_v = -1)$. Let $\tilde{h}_i$ and $\tilde{J}_{ij}$ denote the direction-normalized spin fields and couplings, and let $R_u = \sum_{w \notin \{u, v\}} |\tilde{J}_{uw}|$ and $R_v = \sum_{w \notin \{u, v\}} |\tilde{J}_{vw}|$.

  1. Single-endpoint coupling dominance (:interval, :exact): generalizes Boettcher–Davidheiser (2020) Section II Rule VI to arbitrary local fields by requiring flips of $t_u$ alone to improve both $V_1$ and $V_2$: $-\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} - |\tilde{h}_u| - R_u > 0$ (:strong) or $\ge 0$ (:weak with allow_weak=true).
  2. Glover–Lewis–Kochenberger (2018) Section 3 Theorems 3.1–3.4 cross-endpoint pair rule (:glk_pair): translated from GLK's maximization of $\sum_i c_i x_i + \sum_{i \ne j} Q_{ij} x_i x_j$ (with symmetric off-diagonal $Q_{ij} = Q_{ji} = q_{ij}/2$) to normalized minimization in spin coordinates, and requiring a favoring coupling $\text{center} = -\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} > 0$:
    • Branch A rules out $V_1$ by flipping $t_u \to +\sigma$ (half-margin $m_u(V_1) = -\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} - \sigma \tilde{h}_u - R_u$) and $V_2$ by flipping $t_v \to +1$ (half-margin $m_v(V_2) = -\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} - \tilde{h}_v - R_v$), mapping both violating states directly to $(+\sigma, +1)$ in one flip.
    • Branch B rules out $V_1$ by flipping $t_v \to -1$ (half-margin $m_v(V_1) = -\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} + \tilde{h}_v - R_v$) and $V_2$ by flipping $t_u \to -\sigma$ (half-margin $m_u(V_2) = -\sigma \tilde{J}_{uv} + \sigma \tilde{h}_u - R_u$), mapping both violating states directly to $(-\sigma, -1)$ in one flip.
    Taking the branch with the larger lower bound $\text{lo}_{\text{glk}}$, $\text{lo}_{\text{glk}} > 0$ certifies :strong persistency and $\text{lo}_{\text{glk}} == 0$ (when allow_weak=true and $\text{center} > 0$) certifies :weak persistency.
  3. Explicitly deferred GLK rules: single-branch inequality implications ($x_u + x_v \ge 1$, $x_u + x_v \le 1$, $x_u \le x_v$ when only one of $V_1, V_2$ is ruled out) do not induce an unconstrained parity substitution $s_u = \pm s_v$ without auxiliary slack variables or penalty terms, and GLK Section 4 multi-neighbor matrix rules require higher-order neighborhood scans; both are explicitly deferred outside unconstrained quadratic pair contraction.
using QUBOPreprocessing
import QUBOTools as QT
ws = Workspace(QT.Model(Dict(:x=>3.0,:y=>1.0,:isolate=>0.0),
                        Dict((:x,:y)=>-2.0)); rules=:dominance)
root = checkpoint(ws)
first = propagate!(ws; max_deductions=1)
@assert first.stop_reason == :deduction_limit
@assert verify_certificate(ws,export_certificate(ws)).valid
last = propagate!(ws)
@assert last.stop_reason == :quiescent
@assert isempty(assumptions(ws))
@assert length(deductions(ws)) == 2
result = materialize(ws)
@assert verify_certificate(ws,result.certificate.proof).valid
@assert QT.variables(export_model(result)) == [:isolate]
deductions(ws), last

Incremental work and interruption

Construction prepares exact Rational{BigInt} fields and derivative-bound caches once, plus an initial original-index-order queue. Unlike the standalone prove_dominance interval/fallback helper, propagation reads its exact caches and records method=:exact. Installing one deduction updates only its free neighbors and queues each dirty neighbor once. No QUBOTools constructor, full conversion, source copy or proof export occurs per deduction. Bit complexity is additional to structural counts. After the initial scan, an unchanged call performs no candidate or adjacency work. Genuine cascades may touch the graph.

Per-call limits are nonnegative machine-sized integers:

LimitCharged work
max_workcandidate checks plus deduction-update adjacency visits
max_deductionscommitted deductions
max_exact_workcached sign checks and conservative reservations of one constant plus three exact values per incident edge for an attempted update
deadline_nsabsolute UInt64 monotonic time_ns() deadline

A complete update is reserved before commitment. Fixed-neighbor skips can make exact reservations conservative. If a deduction cannot fit, its queue entry remains pending; its examined candidate/sign check is charged again on resume. Deadline checks surround preparation and run per incident edge; the short commit/queue-journal boundary finishes without interruption. These are algorithmic limits, not hard real-time or asynchronous process-cancellation guarantees.

Reports use :work_limit, :deduction_limit, :verification_limit, :deadline or :quiescent. Every stop retains a sound committed prefix. A later call continues the pending queue. pending includes queued entries already fixed by a cascade until they are consumed. Report totals count executed/reserved propagation work even if the model is later rolled back. Initialization, caller updates, replay, explicit export, verifier and solver costs are separate.

Queue consumption and fixings both have undo records. Rollback restores exact fields, bounds, constant, pending cursor/flags, caller and deduction provenance. changes(ws, cp) returns caller pins; deductions(ws; since=cp) returns only inferences. Both inspect fixing records, skipping queue-only history. Checkpoints are constant-size, and stale branches/resets are rejected. Arbitrary unfix! conservatively discards deductions and replays only retained explicit callers; it is a documented full rebuild. Resume propagation explicitly afterward.

Proof and consumer boundaries

export_certificate(ws) explicitly copies the original source and ordered proof chain, or returns nothing in constant-size work when there are no deductions. Each step records its caller-prefix length: adding a later assumption does not rewrite an earlier bound or constant. The independent verifier substitutes the birth scope and validated predecessor chain in the original polynomial. A root-global step has caller-prefix length zero. Captured certificates require the exact current trail tip; historical branch evidence is rejected after rollback, replay or reset even if the same assignments are reapplied.

materialize captures an owned residual, label map, complete fixed assignment and proof chain. Root deductions have certificate.scope == :original; explicit caller pins produce :assumptions. Caller pins do not prove the branch contains a global optimum of the original problem. Default Float64 export still requires exactly representable residual coefficients/constants; otherwise request coefficient_type=Rational{BigInt} and a compatible consumer. Certified inference does not authorize rounding. Reconstruction validates every free residual value and restores all fixed values in original order. With no free variables, the zero-dimensional residual is handled locally with an empty child state. When free variables remain, propagation does not prove an arbitrary state optimal.

Bounded evidence

The exhaustive tests independently enumerate original/scoped optima on guarded tiny fixtures, compare every residual energy, verify all deductions, and check binary/spin equivalence. Sparse local-change tests compare n=10 and n=2000 work/allocation shape without wall-time thresholds. Run examples/dominance.jl for the resumable cascade and scripts/dominance_pilot.jl for a tiny paired on/off CPU pilot. The pilot charges workspace construction, propagation, proof capture/verification, model export, the same bounded exhaustive child, reconstruction and audit. It retains every paired repetition and separates phases. Reduced variables/edges and total costs are reported together, including no-reduction controls. This pilot is not the accepted operating envelope or an ecosystem adoption decision; those remain with QUBOBenchmarks issue #30.

The retained source artifact docs/evidence/dominance-pilot.md records the producing revision and all paired raw rows. In that tiny warm run, the all-fixed case reduced total time, while the cascade and both no-reduction controls were slower with preprocessing. Variable reduction alone did not predict payoff.

For degree-0/1/2 local elimination (eliminate_low_degree!, examples/elimination.jl, scripts/elimination_pilot.jl, and retained artifact docs/evidence/elimination-pilot.md with raw rows in docs/evidence/elimination-pilot.tsv), the per-degree queues elim_queues[1..4] track only vertices with active degree 0..3 so calls with a smaller max_degree cutoff leave higher-degree buckets untouched for subsequent calls with a larger max_degree without any rescan:

  • Dense / high-degree no-benefit control (dense_k5, 5-vertex K_5): elim_queues are initially empty (0 candidates examined, 0 edge visits, 32 -> 32 states). End-to-end on (including workspace construction and residual export) takes 1100.78 µs (401.4 KiB) vs 650.26 µs (327.5 KiB) off (median paired +431.42 µs).
  • Tiny sparse instances (whisker_5, 4-clique core + 1 leaf, n = 5 -> 4): eliminating 1 degree-<= 2 vertex halves the exhaustive child search (32 -> 16 states), but workspace construction, exact Rational{BigInt} table certification, proof verification and residual materialization take 481.00 µs (255.2 KiB) vs 287.09 µs (257.4 KiB) off (median paired +199.98 µs).
  • Break-even and sparse payoff (whisker_7, whisker_10, and cycle_8): each eliminated degree-<= 2 vertex halves the downstream exponential search space at O(touched) local cost (<= 4 boundary evaluations per vertex), crossing end-to-end break-even against the exact-enumeration child at 3 eliminated variables (whisker_7, 128 -> 16 states, 1824.74 -> 1054.68 µs, median paired −754.36 µs, 1274.6 -> 353.7 KiB), reaching a ~14x total time and ~27x allocation reduction at 6 eliminated variables (whisker_10, 1024 -> 16 states, 29738.92 -> 2094.45 µs, median paired −18940.16 µs, 13622.6 -> 502.2 KiB), and solving cycle_8 completely where strict dominance removes 0 variables (256 -> 1 state, 2920.62 -> 1436.53 µs, median paired −1518.64 µs).
